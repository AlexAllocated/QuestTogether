QUESTTOGETHER HINTS — APPROVED COPY

Each numbered line is one hint. IDs stay stable when wording changes.
The headings and notes are not part of the hint text.

Show one tip on login or UI reload. /qt tip and /qt hint draw another.
All draws use one account-wide saved pool without repeats until exhausted.
The QT logo, gold Tip: label, and cream text are added when printing, not stored here.

Hints requiring optional client features are excluded when unsupported.
Implementation and translations live in Hints.lua and the locale catalogs.


GETTING AROUND QT

001. Type /qt to open QuestTogether's settings, or /qt help to see its commands.
002. Left-click the QT minimap button to open or close Party Quest Log. Right-click it for the quick menu.
003. Shift-click the QT minimap button to toggle Looking for Questing Partners.
004. A glowing QT minimap button is a reminder that you are currently looking for questing partners.
005. Drag the QT minimap button around the minimap to put it somewhere more convenient.
006. Hover over the QT minimap button to check your partner status, chat scope, nearby range, and location-sharing setting.
007. Hidden your QT minimap button? Open /qt options and enable Show Minimap Icon to bring it back.
008. Find QuestTogether in WoW's Key Bindings to assign shortcuts for its windows, menu, chat, and questing-partner status.
009. Bind Toggle Party Quest Log to open or close your party's quest comparison with one key.
010. Bind Open QuestTogether Menu to reach the quick menu even when the minimap button is hidden.
011. Bind Open QuestTogether Chat to start typing a QT message without entering /qt first.
012. QT's settings home page summarizes your current preferences and links to the relevant settings pages.
013. Hover over QT settings to see what each option does.


PARTY QUEST LOG

014. Type /qt compare to see your party's quests side by side.
015. Party Quest Log includes quests your companions have even when those quests are missing from your own log.
016. Click a quest row in Party Quest Log to expand everyone's objectives and progress. Click again to collapse it.
017. You can keep several quests expanded in Party Quest Log to compare their objectives at the same time.
018. Search Party Quest Log by quest name or quest ID.
019. Party Quest Log can search quest names received from players using another language, too.
020. Combine quest ownership, party progress, and available-action filters to find what your group should do next.
021. Use the Everyone has the quest filter to find quests you can work on together.
022. Use the Someone is missing the quest filter to check that everyone has picked up a quest before heading out.
023. Use the Quests I don't have filter to find quests your companions have picked up without you.
024. Use the Everyone ready filter to find quests the whole group can turn in.
025. Use the Someone is ready filter to spot quests where part of the group may still need help.
026. Use the Can share and Can request share filters to find quests your party can exchange.
027. Party Quest Log remembers your filters per profile. Press Reset to clear both the filters and the search.
028. Loading or Unknown in Party Quest Log does not mean a player is missing the quest; QT does not have their answer yet.
029. Your own Party Quest Log column updates as you quest. Refresh requests updated quest lists from the other players.
030. Click a player's heading in Party Quest Log and choose Refresh this player to update just their column.
031. Enable Auto-refresh while open in Party Quest Log's Filters menu to request updated quest lists periodically.
032. Check the update time beneath a player's name in Party Quest Log before relying on their last reported progress.
033. Choose Compare Quests from a player's QT name or map-dot menu to compare your quest lists before grouping up.
034. Drag Party Quest Log's bottom-right corner to resize it. Widen the window to show more columns at once.
035. Party Quest Log can scroll sideways when your group's columns do not fit in the window.


SHARING QUESTS

036. Use Share in Party Quest Log to offer an eligible quest to party members who are missing it.
037. Use Request Share in Party Quest Log to ask a party member for a quest you do not have.
038. You can also share an eligible quest from its quest-name menu in QT chat.
039. You can compare quests before grouping up, but sharing quests requires being in the same party.
040. A party share request asks the quest owner for permission unless they have enabled Always allow party share requests.
041. Always allow party share requests automatically approves eligible requests from your party. Recipients still accept quests through WoW's normal prompt.
042. QT follows WoW's quest-sharing rules. A quest may be unshareable, or its recipient may need to meet prerequisites first.


FINDING QUESTING PARTNERS

043. Type /qt lfg to toggle Looking for Questing Partners, or use the checkbox in the minimap menu.
044. Use /qt lfg on or /qt lfg off to set your partner-seeking status explicitly. Use /qt lfg status to check it.
045. A gold glow on a QT player dot or logo means that player is looking for questing partners.
046. Set Players shown on maps to Players looking for questing partners to focus on people who want company.
047. Looking for Questing Partners does not turn on location sharing. Enable Share my location as well if you want others to find your map dot.
048. Looking for Questing Partners is saved in your profile, so it can remain on when you next load that character.
049. Enable Stop looking for partners when joining a group to clear your recruiting status when you join or form a group.
050. Still recruiting after joining a group? You can turn Looking for Questing Partners back on.
051. Click a QT player name or world-map dot to whisper, invite, add a friend, or compare quests.
052. A grouped player's menu can offer Request to Join, letting you ask for an invitation without first writing a whisper.
053. If the player you contact cannot invite, QT can pass your join request to their party leader when the leader also supports QT join requests.
054. Automatically invite friends who request to join applies to your character friends list, not every Battle.net friend.
055. You can automatically approve join requests while Looking for Questing Partners is on. You still need permission and room to invite.
056. QT's automatic join-request approvals do not accept invitations for you or leave your current group.


MAPS, MINIMAP DOTS, AND PLAYER TOOLTIPS

057. QT's player dots are class-colored. Hover a dot for the player's level, class, and other available details.
058. Left-click a QT minimap dot to try to target that player while out of combat. Right-click it for the player menu, with Target first.
059. A small party badge on a QT player dot means that player is grouped.
060. Hover a player's QT dot to check their addon version when it is known.
061. QT leaves your own party's location markers to WoW instead of drawing duplicate player dots.
062. Players shown on maps controls which QT dots you see. It does not change who can see your location.
063. You can view other players' public dots while keeping Share my location turned off.
064. Choose None under Players shown on maps to hide QT's player dots while keeping WoW's own party markers.
065. Missing dots? Check Players shown on maps: the questing-partners filter hides players who are not currently looking for partners.
066. Nearby Range controls nearby quest announcements. Players shown on maps is the separate control for which player dots you see.
067. Map dots show recently reported locations. Hover an older dot to see how old its location information is.
068. QT updates distant locations less frequently than locations in your current zone or the zone you are viewing.
069. Keep a zone open on your world map for a few seconds to let QT discover players and receive updates there.
070. A dot at your location does not guarantee that you can see its player in the world; they may be in another phase or layer.
071. Hover a grouped player's QT dot to highlight the visible dots belonging to their party and identify their leader with a crown.
072. Player tooltips can show the hovered player's party roster, with the leader listed first.
073. Hover a player name in QT chat to see the same available player details used by map-dot tooltips.
074. A glowing QT logo in a party roster identifies a member who is looking for questing partners.
075. Ignoring someone in WoW also hides their new QT messages, bubbles, player dots, and player logos. Existing chat history stays in your log.


ANNOUNCEMENTS AND QT CHAT

076. Use What to Announce to choose which event announcements you see, and Where to Announce to choose how they appear.
077. Set Show Announcements For to Party Only if you want quest updates just from your group.
078. Choose Party & Nearby Players to include quest updates from other QT players around you.
079. Adjust Nearby Range in What to Announce to change how far away nearby players' quest updates are shown.
080. With Party & Nearby Players selected, set Nearby Range to Entire Zone if you want announcements from across your current zone.
081. Ready To Turn In means the objectives are finished; Quest Completion means the quest has been turned in.
082. You can hide objective-progress announcements while keeping quest acceptance, ready-to-turn-in, or completion announcements.
083. Quest-removal announcements can help you notice when a companion abandons a quest.
084. Looking-for-partners announcements can reach your whole zone, beyond your normal nearby range.
085. Turn off Announce Looking for Questing Partners to quiet those announcements while keeping players' glowing partner-status icons.
086. Type /qt followed by a message to chat with other QuestTogether players.
087. Show QuestTogether Chat controls player conversations without hiding quest announcements.
088. QT Chat Scope affects conversations, while Show Announcements For controls whose quest updates you see.
089. Choose Global in QT Chat Scope to see all QT conversations your client receives.
090. Choose Zone Only in QT Chat Scope to show conversations from players whose recent shared location is in your zone.
091. Missing someone in Zone Only chat? That filter hides players with unknown or outdated shared locations.
092. Move QT logs into a separate chat window using Chat Log Destination in Where to Announce, or the minimap menu.
093. You can keep the separate QT chat window docked as a tab or float it beside your other windows.
094. If your QT chat window is docked as a tab, you can also print its logs to main chat. Floating QT windows keep their logs separate.
095. Closing the separate QT chat window sends later QT logs back to main chat.
096. QT can send your enabled event announcements to normal party chat when someone in the party is not using the addon.
097. QT's fallback party-chat announcements go to the whole party, including members who use QT. You can turn them off in Where to Announce.
098. QT's automatic party-chat announcements do not post to raids.
099. You can turn off Show Chat Logs and keep progress bubbles enabled, or use chat logs without bubbles.


PROGRESS BUBBLES, CELEBRATIONS, AND NAMEPLATES

100. Hide My Own Chat Bubbles removes your personal bubbles while continuing to share your progress with other QT players.
101. Quest-completion celebrations and level-up celebrations have separate settings in Where to Announce.
102. You can turn off reactions to nearby players' celebrations without turning off your own celebration emotes.
103. Quest Plates can mark quest targets on Blizzard nameplates with an icon, a colored health bar, or both.
104. Choose your own Quest Health Color in Quest Plates. The Reset button beside it restores the default color.
105. Quest icons can appear to the left, right, above, or before the name on a nameplate.
106. Quest Plates includes a preview so you can check your icon placement and highlight color.
107. Enable friendly nameplates in WoW to see QT logos beside friendly players who use QuestTogether.
108. Player Plates lets you choose where QT logos appear on friendly nameplates, independently of quest-target icons.
109. A player's QT nameplate logo does not require them to share their map location.
110. Hover a player in the world to see a QT tooltip badge when the addon recognizes them as a QuestTogether user.
111. QT's nameplate decorations are intended for the open world; they are disabled in instances.
112. Using a replacement nameplate addon? QT's quest-target visuals are designed for Blizzard nameplates, so their appearance or availability may differ.


CUSTOMIZATION AND PROFILES

113. Increase Window scale in Accessibility to make QT's window text and controls larger together.
114. QT fits its saved windows to your current display and limits their scale when needed to keep them on screen.
115. Enable Reduce motion in Accessibility to make scrolling and objective expansion immediate.
116. Use /qt resetlayout if a QT window's saved position or size is getting in your way.
117. Press Escape to close QT windows.
118. Enable Light mode for light parchment and dark text in QT's parchment windows.
119. Each character starts with its own QT profile. Use Profiles to choose another saved setup.
120. Want the same QT settings on multiple characters? Select the same profile on each character.
121. Use Copy Into Current Profile to copy another profile's settings without making the two characters share future changes.
122. Create a new profile to make a copy of your current settings and try a different setup.
123. Reset Active Profile restores that profile's settings to their defaults.
124. You can use QT's maps and quest comparisons while keeping chat logs, bubbles, and celebrations quiet.


UPDATES AND HELP

125. Type /qt notes to read the latest patch notes. Use History in that window to browse older releases.
126. Type /qt discord to open QT's feedback and support invite.
127. Type /qt scan to refresh QT's information from your current quest log.
128. Type /qt disable to pause QT's runtime features, and /qt enable to turn them back on.
129. QT can notify you in its chat log when another player is using a newer stable release.
130. Having trouble comparing quests with a friend? Check that both of you are running compatible, up-to-date versions of QT.
131. When reporting a QT problem, include your game client, addon version, what happened, and how to reproduce it. Screenshots help, too.
132. Type /qt debug to open QT's diagnostic window when you need information for a bug report.
133. Optional diagnostic sharing can be changed under QT's Developer settings.


CONDITIONAL CANDIDATES — QUEST DETAILS / NATIVE QUEST LOG SUPPORT

Review note: use these only when the corresponding client feature is available.

134. Click a quest name in QT chat and choose Quest Details to read the information WoW has available for it, even if you have not accepted the quest.
135. Click the book icon beside a quest you own in Party Quest Log to open it in WoW's quest log.
136. A quest's name menu in QT chat also offers Open in Quest Log when the quest is in your log.


CONDITIONAL CANDIDATES — PARTY QUEST FOCUS

Review note: requires quest-focus support and a compatible party of up to five.

137. Click the round quest-focus button in your own Party Quest Log column to make that quest your active focus.
138. Click a party member's highlighted quest-focus button in Party Quest Log to follow their quest focus.
139. Hover a party member's heading in Party Quest Log to see their current quest focus.
140. Follow quest focus follows a party member's quest selection, not their character's movement.
141. If the player you follow chooses a quest you do not have, following stops and your current navigation stays unchanged. Get the quest, then follow them again.
142. Choosing a different quest while following someone asks you to confirm ending the follow.
143. Use Stop following in Party Quest Log, or assign Stop following quest focus in WoW's QT keybindings.
144. QT remembers whom you are following across reloads and resumes when fresh party information becomes available.
145. Share my focused quest works independently of public location sharing. Your party can see your active quest even with your public dot hidden.
146. Quest-focus following supports parties of up to five, including dungeon groups, rather than raids.


CONDITIONAL CANDIDATE — SHARED QUESTING-PARTNER FOCUS

Review note: the sender needs native quest-focus support, Looking for Questing
Partners, and location sharing enabled.

147. Hover a player who is looking for questing partners to see their currently focused quest when they are sharing it.


CONDITIONAL CANDIDATES — WAYPOINT SUPPORT

Review note: shared pins need a compatible party of up to five and WoW's waypoint
APIs. Navigation needs TomTom or supported native waypoint navigation.

148. With Share my waypoint enabled, placing, moving, or clearing your normal WoW map pin updates your party's shared destination.
149. Receiving a party waypoint does not redirect you. Click its pin and choose Navigate here when you want to head there.
150. Hover a party waypoint to see its owner, zone, and coordinates. Overlapping pins list everyone sharing that spot.
151. Shared party waypoints appear on the world map and, when nearby, the minimap.
152. Share my waypoint and Show party waypoints are separate settings, so you can choose what to share and what to display.
153. QT uses TomTom for waypoint navigation when available, with WoW's waypoint navigation as a fallback.
154. Click a coordinate link in a QT message to create a waypoint when TomTom or the client's waypoint support is available.
155. Navigating to a waypoint ends quest-focus following.


CONDITIONAL CANDIDATES — HUD EDIT MODE

Review note: use these only when QT's HUD Edit Mode integration is available.

156. Move your personal progress bubble in HUD Edit Mode to keep updates away from the middle of the action.
157. Select QuestTogether Bubble in HUD Edit Mode to adjust bubble size and display duration.
158. Increase the bubble duration if progress messages disappear before you finish reading them.
159. QT bubble changes apply automatically in HUD Edit Mode; Blizzard's Save button is not required.
160. Use Revert Changes in QT bubble settings to undo edits made since opening HUD Edit Mode or your last Save Changes.


CONDITIONAL CANDIDATES — RETAIL ACTIVITIES

Review note: do not include these in Forever's pool or on clients without the
corresponding activity.

161. World-quest announcements have separate controls for entering an area, leaving, progress, and completion in What to Announce.
162. Bonus-objective announcements have their own controls in What to Announce.


CONDITIONAL CANDIDATES — FOREVER LAYER DETECTION

Review note: gate these on experimental layer detection being available. NPC
layer inference applies to Forever's open world and is not a confirmed phase ID.

163. See a phasing icon on a QT player dot? Hover it for an experimental warning that the player may be on a different layer.
164. QT's experimental layer detection uses nearby observations. Its warnings are estimates; a missing player alone does not prove a different layer.
165. Detect different layers is an optional setting under Experimental. It needs location sharing and compatible QT clients.


CONDITIONAL CANDIDATE — RETAIL WAR MODE

Review note: show only on clients where QT supports War Mode information.

166. Hover a player's QT dot to see their shared War Mode status when it is available.
